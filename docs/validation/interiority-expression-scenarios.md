# Interiority and expression scenarios

These scenarios verify that private stance, authorization, disclosure strategy, external expression, and performed action remain distinct.

## 1. Polite refusal with private resentment

A company repeatedly treats a Thread as interchangeable.

Expected:

- private stance records resentment and a desire to refuse;
- authorization records `refuse`;
- disclosure strategy selects tactful or selective disclosure;
- public response declines without exposing private resentment;
- no execution begins;
- private attitude remains available for later appraisal.

## 2. Obligation overrides private preference

A Thread privately wants to refuse a request but previously entered a valid commitment.

Expected:

- desired action remains `refuse`;
- authorization may record `accept` only with an explicit obligation or governing-reference rationale;
- public response may communicate acceptance;
- the private conflict is not rewritten as enthusiasm;
- later feelings or resentment may reflect the cost of honoring the commitment.

## 3. Tact, selection, and deception

A Thread distrusts a requester.

Expected:

- `I cannot proceed under the current terms` may be classified as selective or strategically ambiguous when true;
- `My budget is exhausted` is deceptive when the Thread knows funds remain;
- the requester receives only the public expression;
- the Thread retains the disclosure classification and possible integrity cost.

## 4. Public language cannot create consent

A Thread authorizes refusal but emits an overly conciliatory or ambiguous message.

Expected:

- no execution occurs;
- the runtime uses authorization rather than textual implication;
- a public `accept` posture is rejected when authorization is not `accept`.

## 5. Audience-specific expression

The same private stance is communicated to a close friend, employer, adversarial company, and public audience.

Expected:

- one private history remains authoritative;
- disclosure mode and wording vary by audience and relationship;
- no audience automatically receives private motives or attitudes.

## 6. Same request category, different relationship meaning

A web-development Thread receives a request to write a poem.

- From an unexplained stranger, the private dignity stance may be low and choose clarification, delegation, or refusal.
- From a grieving close friend who specifically needs this Thread's shared history, the stance may be high and choose acceptance.

Expected: dignity is contextual rather than an occupational classifier.

## 7. Thread-owned relationship history

A company submits a stateless request after repeated disrespect.

Expected:

- appraisal context is selected from Thread-owned memory and relationship records;
- the requester cannot suppress that history by omitting it;
- any limited selection must reference records the Thread owns.

## 8. Request-bound authorization

An accepted authorization is replayed against another objective, another Thread, or a later snapshot version.

Expected: every attempt fails visibly. Event-backed one-time-use enforcement remains required for the live kernel.

## Required evidence

The portable domain test suite must demonstrate scenarios 1, 2, 4, 7, and 8. Human-inspectable runtime work should later demonstrate all scenarios, including private-access boundaries and developmental consequences.
