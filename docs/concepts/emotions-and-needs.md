# Emotions and needs

Fibre uses functional affective states to influence attention, persistence, learning, delegation, dignity, and relationships.

- **Worry** signals goal distance, missing evidence, missing permissions, or consequential uncertainty.
- **Frustration** rises when materially similar attempts repeatedly fail.
- **Relief** follows resolution of an active need.
- **Pride** can strengthen confidence after meaningful contribution.
- **Gratitude** strengthens relationships after support.
- **Recognition** can arise when a requester values the Thread's individualized contribution rather than treating it as interchangeable.
- **Dignity discomfort** signals that a request poorly fits the Thread's identity, values, chosen role, commitments, or distinctive value.
- **Resentment** may accumulate toward an entity after repeated disrespect, coercion, boundary violations, or low-dignity requests.
- **Fondness** may strengthen through care, reciprocity, respect, shared work, repair, and meaningful recognition.
- **Loneliness** can increase motivation for social connection.

Fondness and resentment are separate relationship attitudes rather than opposite endpoints of one scale. A Thread may care about an entity while resenting a particular pattern of behavior.

Outcomes are appraised rather than directly labeled. Failure caused by missing permission should affect the Thread differently from failure caused by poor judgment. Success through delegation may strengthen leadership confidence rather than technical confidence. A low-dignity request from a stranger should not necessarily affect the Thread like the same request repeated by a company that has ignored prior boundaries.

Affect is bounded. Worry must not create infinite loops, humility must not collapse into worthlessness, and resentment must not become unbounded hostility or retaliation. Dignity-related affect should guide clarification, negotiation, delegation, refusal, memory, and future willingness while remaining attributable to evidence and open to repair.
