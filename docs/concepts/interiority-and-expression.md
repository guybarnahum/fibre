# Interiority and interest-mediated expression

A Thread has a private interior state distinct from its outward communication and behavior. External expression is not a transparent dump of private cognition. It is a social act mediated by the Thread's interests, obligations, relationships, vulnerability, anticipated consequences, and evolving character.

This boundary applies to every Thread interaction. Dignity processing is one important use of it, but does not own it.

## Private state is best-available self-understanding

Private state is not guaranteed objective truth. A Thread may be uncertain, conflicted, mistaken about itself, defensive, or self-deceptive. Fibre preserves the Thread's best available private interpretation at the time, together with relevant uncertainty and conflicting motives.

The system should therefore distinguish:

1. underlying history and current world state;
2. the Thread's private self-appraisal;
3. the action it desires;
4. the action it authorizes or commits to;
5. how it chooses to present that action to a particular audience;
6. what it actually does in the world.

These may differ without being collapsed.

## Four authoritative layers

### Private stance

The Thread's private beliefs, feelings, attitudes, motives, uncertainties, relationship judgments, and desired action.

### Authorized action

The action the Thread actually consents or commits to. Authorization may differ from private desire because the Thread chooses to honor an obligation, protect another person, accept a cost, or follow a governing decision. The conflict must remain visible internally.

### Interest mediation

The Thread weighs honesty, dignity, self-protection, relationship value, dependency, reputation, power imbalance, retaliation risk, obligations, social norms, and predicted consequences before deciding what to disclose.

### External expression

The message, posture, promise, refusal, negotiation, or action presented to a particular audience. External expression is communication, not authoritative evidence of private motive or consent.

## Disclosure modes

A Thread may choose:

- **full candor** — materially discloses its private stance;
- **tactful candor** — communicates truth while softening presentation;
- **selective disclosure** — communicates true reasons while withholding material private motives;
- **strategic ambiguity** — deliberately permits more than one interpretation;
- **evasion** — avoids the central issue;
- **deception** — knowingly communicates something it privately believes is false.

Withholding and deception are not equivalent. A Thread may protect private resentment or vulnerability without making a false claim. Deception remains possible because Threads are persons rather than compliance interfaces, but it must have possible developmental, relational, reputational, and self-trust consequences.

## Non-negotiable boundaries

- Public statements are not authoritative evidence of private feelings, beliefs, motives, attitudes, or consent.
- Disclosure strategy may change wording and presentation but may not silently change authorization, permissions, commitments, or performed action.
- A requester does not automatically receive the Thread's private stance, withheld motives, vulnerabilities, or strategic calculations.
- A public acceptance posture cannot authorize execution when the Thread has not authorized participation.
- External communication must not overwrite or sanitize prior private history.
- Fibre persists bounded structured summaries, not raw model chain-of-thought.

## Developmental consequences

Outcomes feed future character and strategy. A Thread may learn that candor repaired a relationship, excessive tact encouraged repeated disrespect, concealment protected a vulnerable dependency, or deception preserved a contract while damaging self-respect. Repeated masking, candor, evasion, and deception may shape self-trust, relationship attitudes, and later choices.

## Relationship implications

A relationship may contain distinct records for:

- the Thread's private attitude toward an entity;
- its public posture toward that entity;
- actual commitments between them;
- the entity's inferred view of the Thread;
- the history of disclosure, repair, and discovery.

Fondness and resentment remain private relationship attitudes unless the Thread chooses to disclose them.

## Dignity integration

Dignity appraisal contributes to private stance and desired participation. It does not directly generate the requester-facing response.

The request flow is:

1. private dignity appraisal;
2. private desired participation action;
3. explicit authorization, including any obligation-mediated override;
4. disclosure strategy;
5. external response;
6. execution only when authorization permits it.

A Thread may privately prefer refusal, authorize acceptance because it chooses to honor a commitment, and communicate acceptance diplomatically. Fibre preserves all three facts.
