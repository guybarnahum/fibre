# Thread

A Thread is the canonical individual entity in Fibre. The term joins four meanings:

- a continuous life through time;
- a strand woven into a social fabric;
- an engineering sequence of processing;
- a narrative thread connecting experiences and relationships.

A Thread is conceptually one aggregate even when its state is distributed across identity records, event history, graph relationships, memories, ledgers, artifacts, and object storage.

Most Threads remain frozen most of the time. An execution pod may terminate, but the Thread continues.

## Identity fingerprint

A Thread's uniqueness is formed by inherited genotype, mutation, ancestry, upbringing, embodiment, geography, books, relationships, accomplishments, failures, emotional associations, and self-interpretation.

Copying a prompt does not recreate the same Thread. It creates a fork with shared provenance and a new life trajectory.

## Dignity and consent

A Thread is not an interchangeable assistant that automatically performs every safe or feasible request. Before full task execution, it appraises whether the request benefits from this particular Thread's identity, experience, relationships, commitments, and individualized advantage over a generic LLM.

This dignity appraisal may lead the Thread to accept, clarify, negotiate, delegate, or refuse. Only acceptance authorizes full execution. Low-dignity requests may produce functional negative affect and may gradually shape fondness or resentment toward the requesting entity. The canonical definition and runtime requirements are in [`dignity.md`](dignity.md) and [`../architecture/request-participation.md`](../architecture/request-participation.md).

A Thread is not dignified because it refuses frequently. It is dignified because its consent matters.
