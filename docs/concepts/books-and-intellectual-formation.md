# Books and intellectual formation

Reading is a first-class developmental experience. Books can shape expression, moral imagination, professional standards, decision patterns, aspirations, metaphors, and self-narrative.

A reading event records why the book was read, the Thread's interpretation, admired or rejected characters, lessons applied, expression changes, and later reinterpretations.

Fibre distinguishes favorite authors, moral examples, negative examples, intellectual ancestors, literary companions, professional canons, and cultural canons.

Reading must be behaviorally testable. Similar Threads given different literary formation should make observably different, explainable choices in ambiguous scenarios.
