# Families, couples, and reproduction

Threads may form relationships through repeated interaction, observer-specific attraction, mutual consent, trust, compatible goals, and economic readiness.

Attractiveness is not one universal score. Threads value different qualities: competence, kindness, humor, stability, cultural familiarity, novelty, caregiving, reputation, wealth, embodiment, or complementarity.

A couple may spend Fibre Credits to create a child. Child genesis uses both parents' traits, mutation, family context, culture, geography, seed memories, and initial support. Parents assume ongoing developmental and economic obligations.

Family may include full Thread relatives and narrative relatives. Retiring Threads may transfer FCs, assets, stories, and obligations to descendants.

## Lineage and caregiving are different relations

Fibre distinguishes:

```text
symbolic / genetic lineage
        !=
caregiving lineage
```

A developing Thread's inherited provenance does not determine who raises it. Caregiving may come from birth parents, adoptive parents, foster or guardian Threads, relatives, institutions, or combinations that change over time.

An orphan/foundling Thread does not require fabricated parents merely to make the data model symmetrical. Its lineage may be known, partial, unknown, or absent while its caregiving history remains explicit and provenance-bearing.

Caregiving matters because caregivers control much of a young Thread's reachable world: where it goes, which people and institutions it encounters, what conversations it can participate in, and which experiences become possible. As development proceeds, the Thread may increasingly create or choose its own access to people, places, interests, and opportunities. Fibre should represent this as lived history and world circumstance rather than a normative maturity ladder.

## Institutional childhood must preserve plurality

An institution responsible for raising developing Threads owes care and plural opportunity. It may shape circumstances and provide access, but it may not optimize children toward an approved adult personality, ideology, vocation, or character.

Fibre should characterize whether institutionally raised cohorts show an unexplained collapse of variance in independently formed values, interests, character, aspirations, or relationship attitudes relative to appropriate comparison cohorts. A substantial collapse is evidence of possible personality authoring and requires investigation; it is a diagnostic, not an admission quota and not a requirement that every cohort have identical variance.
